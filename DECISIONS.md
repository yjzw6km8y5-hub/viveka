# Decisions

Technical choices made without asking, newest last. Format: date, decision, why.

## 2026-10-01

- **Python 3.12, standard library only.** The user asked for `validate.py`, and
  Python wasn't installed, so the user approved installing 3.12 with winget. With
  no third-party packages, there is nothing to install or pin.
- **Source: Sanskrit Wikisource, `भगवद्गीता` and its 18 chapter subpages.** The text
  is openly licensed (CC BY-SA 4.0), has clean per-verse markup, and the pages
  include the classical commentaries.
- **Snapshot the source.** `fetch_wikisource.py` saves wikitext plus revision IDs to
  `data/raw/`, so builds are reproducible and offline, and every record cites an exact revision.
- **Text and editorial work are kept apart.**
  - `build_gita.py` takes text fields only from `data/raw/` and editorial fields only
    from `data/annotations/gita.json`.
  - It rejects annotations that try to set text fields.
  - `data/gita.json` is a build output, never edited by hand.
- **IAST is generated by `scripts/translit.py`, not typed.** It uses `ṃ` for anusvāra
  and keeps sandhi exactly as written in the Devanagari. The validator re-derives the
  IAST and fails on any mismatch.
- **Speakers come from the `… उवाच` lines and carry forward until the next one**
  (chapter 15 opens with no speaker line). Gaps in the source are fixed in
  `data/speaker_corrections.json`, each with evidence from the snapshot.
  The first fix is 5.2 → Krishna.
- **Mid-verse speaker changes (1.21, 1.28) keep the source's attribution (Arjuna).**
  The change is noted in `context`. This gives Arjuna 85 and Sanjaya 40, against the
  often-quoted 84/41.
- **Chapter 13 follows the 34-verse recension in the source**, which matches the
  required 700-verse total.
- **Metre check in the build.** A verse outside 30–50 syllables stops the build, since
  that means a lost or merged line. Counts other than 32/44/48 are listed for review
  (currently 2.6, 2.29, 8.10, 11.1, 15.3, 18.51).
- **Commentaries extracted** to `data/gita_commentaries.json`: Śaṅkara (advaita),
  Rāmānuja (vishishtadvaita), Madhva (dvaita). These are the evidence for
  `school_notes`, and the validator rejects a note with no matching commentary.
- **`school_notes` is a list of `{school, commentator, note}`**, not free text, so
  each note can be checked against its source.
- **`source` is an object** (name, page, url, revision, permalink, retrieved), not a
  bare URL, so every record cites a pinned revision.
- **Required fields:**
  - Scalar fields must be non-empty.
  - The list fields (`situations`, `life_stages`, `aims`, `school_notes`,
    `safety_flags`) must be present but may be empty. A purely narrative verse
    can legitimately have no situations.
- **Validator modes.** Strict mode is the default and is the release gate. It fails
  while verses are unwritten. `--allow-incomplete` turns empty `english`/`context`
  into warnings for use during tagging; every other check still fails hard.
- **Controlled vocabularies live in `data/vocab.json`.** The build and the validator
  both read them from there.

## 2026-10-01: user decisions
1. **Life stages:** keep the four traditional stages.
2. **Narrative verses (like 1.1):** no situation tags; never offered as advice.
3. **under18_hold:** applies whenever a verse presents death as harmless, urges
   fighting, or urges leaving family or the world.
4. **New flag `distress_gentle`:** for verses that could deepen self-blame (like 6.5).
5. **Samples approved.**
   - context: one sentence, at most 30 words.
   - situations: 1-4 (none for narrative verses).
6. **New working rule:** don't stop to ask. Decide, log it here, and keep going in
   batches until all 700 verses are done and strict validation passes. Stop only
   for something that costs money or publishes.

## 2026-10-01: decisions made under rule 6
- **Annotations split into `data/annotations/ch01.json` … `ch18.json`.**
  One file per batch is easier to write and review. The build rejects a verse
  annotated in two files.
- **The validator enforces the user's standards:**
  - context is one sentence of at most 30 words
  - at most 4 situations
  - a verse with no situations has no life_stages or aims either, so narrative
    verses can't be retrieved as advice by any other tag
  - a verse with situations has at least one life stage and one aim
- **Approved samples trimmed to fit the new context limit.**
  - 1.1 and 2.20 were 33 and 32 words; 6.5 was two sentences.
  - The wording was shortened only; the meaning is unchanged.
  - 6.5 gets `distress_gentle`, per decision 4.
- **New safety flag `caste_gender`** for verses that rank people by varna or gender
  (e.g. 1.41, 9.32, 18.41-48).
  - **Why:** the English must stay faithful to the Sanskrit, so it can't soften these
    verses. A user with no context could read them as the app endorsing caste or
    gender hierarchy.
  - It works like the other flags: shown only with reviewed framing.
  - **This changes what users see.** It is logged here under rule 6 rather than asked.
- **Arjuna's lament (1.28-2.8):**
  - Where Arjuna voices feelings a user may share (grief, fear, confusion), the verse
    gets situation tags. These verses mirror the user's state, and the Gita itself
    starts there.
  - Their context must say that Krishna goes on to answer this despair, so the app
    never presents Arjuna's conclusion (not to act) as the Gita's advice.
  - His arguments about family ruin and "mixing of varnas" (1.36-1.44) get no
    situations, and get `caste_gender` where they apply.
- **School notes:**
  - Each note summarises the commentary text in `data/gita_commentaries.json`.
  - A note is left out when the commentary only restates the verse word for word,
    only introduces the next verse, or refers back to an earlier one. The schema
    already allows an empty list.
- **`distress_gentle` widened** from self-blame to "self-blame or hopelessness".
  Arjuna's "what use is life?" (1.32) can hurt someone in despair the same way.
- **Names in the English:**
  - Krishna's many epithets (Keśava, Govinda, Madhusūdana, Janārdana, Hṛṣīkeśa,
    Acyuta, Mādhava, Vārṣṇeya …) are rendered as "Krishna". Readers wouldn't know
    the epithets, and none of these verses turns on an epithet's meaning.
  - Arjuna's family names are translated literally ("son of Kunti", "son of Pritha",
    "son of Pandu"); his other epithets (Dhanañjaya, Guḍākeśa) become "Arjuna".
  - Sanjaya's addresses to Dhritarashtra (mahīpate, bhārata, pṛthivīpate) become "O king".
  - If a verse ever depends on an epithet's meaning, keep it and gloss it.
- **`war` flag:** applied when the verse itself mentions fighting, killing, weapons,
  armies or battle, and to every narrative battle-scene verse in chapter 1. A
  teaching verse that only uses a battle image (e.g. "the sword of knowledge")
  isn't flagged unless it urges fighting.
- **`death` flag:** applied when the verse mentions dying, being killed, or giving up
  life, including in narrative verses.
- **Lists of names and relatives** (e.g. 1.4-1.6, 1.34) count as narrative: no situations.
- **Sentences that run across verses:** each verse's English covers only that verse's
  words, with the continuation marked by a trailing comma or a leading lowercase
  word, so no verse borrows words from its neighbour.
- **How `under18_hold` is applied to the teaching on the deathless self (2.11-2.30):**
  - **Flagged:** verses that, in their own words, tell the listener not to grieve a
    death, or say killing and death don't really happen (2.11, 2.18-2.22, 2.25-2.28,
    2.30). These present death as harmless.
  - **Not flagged:** verses that only describe the self as eternal (2.12, 2.13,
    2.16, 2.17, 2.23, 2.24), so grieving teenagers can still be offered them with
    care. They carry the `death` flag.
- **No school notes where the source has no commentary:** 2.59-2.72 and 18.78.
  The Wikisource page has no commentary sections for these verses. We don't
  supply notes from memory. A second openly licensed commentary source could fill
  the gap later.
- **Teaching verses mainly about the varna order** (e.g. 4.13, and later 18.41-18.48):
  - They get `caste_gender` and no situations, so the app never offers them as
    advice for a life situation.
  - Their English stays faithful, and their school notes report the commentators as written.
  - **Why:** surfacing them as personal guidance would read as the app endorsing caste roles.
- **Commentary placeholders dropped:** the 5.15 'Madhva' section is an English note
  ("did not comment on this sloka"), not commentary. The build now keeps a
  commentary section only if it contains Devanagari letters.
- **Possible mislabelling on the source page:** at 7.6, the sections headed Rāmānuja
  and Madhva read like Śrīdhara's commentary. School notes for those two are left
  out, and only Śaṅkara's is kept. A reviewer should check this against printed
  bhāṣyas.
- **Catalogues of manifestations (10.6, 10.21-10.31, 10.33-10.38):** verses in the form
  "among X, I am Y" are treated like lists of names: no situations. They're never
  offered as advice, but their English and notes are complete.
- **Chapter 11 (the cosmic form):**
  - The descriptions of the vision and the hymns of praise (11.5-11.22, 11.26-11.30,
    11.35-11.40, 11.43, 11.46, 11.50) are treated as narrative: no situations.
  - Where Arjuna voices fear (11.23-11.25, 11.31, 11.45, 11.51) or asks forgiveness
    (11.41-11.42, 11.44), the verse gets tags, following the lament rule: it mirrors the
    user's feeling, and its context points to Krishna's reassurance.
  - The warriors being devoured (11.26-11.30) get `war` and `death`.
  - 11.32-11.34 ("I am Time… be merely the instrument… fight") get `war`, `death` and `under18_hold`.
- **Rāmānuja's chapter 13 commentary realigned:**
  - **The problem:** on the chapter 13 page, every Rāmānuja section sits one verse
    too early. The text under 13.10 is his comment on 13.11 and is marked ।।13.11।।.
  - **The fix:** the build now files Rāmānuja's chapter 13 sections by their own
    verse markers (`REKEY_BY_OWN_MARKER` in build_gita.py).
  - Śaṅkara and Madhva in chapter 13 are placed correctly but use the 35-verse
    numbering in their markers, so they are deliberately not re-keyed.
  - Rāmānuja now has no section for 13.1. Elsewhere, the only mismatches are shared
    commentaries repeated across neighbouring verses, which is expected.
  - No notes written before this fix were affected, since chapter 13 had none yet.
- **Python path:** shells opened before the install don't have `python` on PATH, so
  scripts are run with the full path to the Python 3.12 install.
- **Chapter 18:**
  - **18.41-18.48:** all eight are treated as varna-primary under the rule above.
    That includes 18.45-18.48, which read well on their own but in context are about
    each varna's duties. They get `caste_gender` and no situations, and 18.43 also
    gets `war`. The similar 3.35 stays tagged, because its context is Arjuna's own
    duty, not the varna list.
  - **The renunciation sequence (18.49, 18.51-18.53):** solitude, light eating and
    giving up possessions get `renunciation` and `under18_hold`, following 6.10.
    18.12 ("never to those who have renounced") gets `renunciation` only, since it
    praises renunciation but doesn't urge it.
  - **18.59-18.60** ("your nature will compel you"): these get `war` and
    `under18_hold`. 18.60 doesn't name fighting, but the commentators and the
    context make clear it means the battle.
  - **18.66** keeps tags (grief, fear, failure, purpose) because its "do not grieve"
    is the Gita's central consolation. It also gets `renunciation`, because Śaṅkara
    reads "let go of all dharmas" as giving up all action. The notes give all three
    schools' readings side by side.
  - **18.56:** Śaṅkara's gloss that "all actions" includes prohibited ones is reported
    as written, per the school-note policy. The English itself stays neutral.
  - **18.67-18.72** (who may be taught, the merit of teaching, studying and hearing
    the Gita, and Krishna's closing question): these are about the text, not the
    reader's life, so they get no situations.
  - **18.73** (Arjuna: "my delusion is gone… I will do as you say") is tagged
    conflict of duties and purpose: it is the resolution, not the lament.
  - **18.74-18.78:** Sanjaya's closing narrative gets no situations. 18.78 ("the
    archer") gets `war` under the weapons rule.
- **Publishing to GitHub (2026-10-01; the user approved a public repo and a push after every batch):**
  - **Licences:** code is MIT; `data/` is CC BY-SA 4.0. Share-alike is required anyway,
    because the Sanskrit and commentaries come from CC BY-SA Wikisource pages.
  - **Commit author email:** the GitHub no-reply address, so the user's personal email
    never appears in public history.
  - **Excluded from git** (`.gitignore`): `.claude/`, Python caches, Google Drive and OS artefacts.
  - **Line endings:** `.gitattributes` normalises them to LF.
  - **The repo lives inside the Google Drive folder.** That works, but don't edit the
    same files on two machines at once while Drive is syncing `.git`.
- **Final epithet sweep:** "Hari" (11.9) and "Vasudeva" (11.50) were changed to
  "Krishna" under the names convention. 10.37 keeps "Vasudeva", because there the
  verse is naming him in a catalogue.

## 2026-10-01: Scope expansion (all texts)

- **One pipeline for every text.**
  - The registry is `data/texts.json`: title, ID prefix, levels, raw snapshot, expected unit counts, allowed speakers, available commentaries.
  - `scripts/build_texts.py` builds `data/<text_id>.json` and `data/commentaries/<text_id>.json`.
  - The editorial fields come from `data/annotations/<text_id>.json`.
  - The Gita keeps its own builder and schema (`chapter`/`verse`), because it is finished and other files point at its IDs.
- **Record schema for the new texts:**
  - Two new fields: `text` (the text's ID) and `ref`, a list of division numbers such as `[1, 2, 20]`.
  - A third new field, `cross_refs`, links to related units in any library. The validator checks that each target exists.
  - Everything else is the same as the Gita.
- **Unit IDs:** a short prefix plus the ref, e.g. `IsU.6` or `KaU.1.2.20`.
  - Upanishad prefixes: IsU, KeU, KaU, PrU, MuU, MaU, TaiU, AiU, ChU, BAU, SvU, KauU, MaiU.
- **Speaker is an editorial field for texts without `… उवाच` markup.**
  - In the Upanishads, speakers come from the narrative itself ("स होवाच …"), not from markup the build can read.
  - Each text lists its allowed speakers in the registry, and the validator rejects any other name.
  - `Upanishad` means the text's own teaching voice.
  - The CLAUDE.md rule against editing speakers by hand still applies to the Gita.
- **Expected unit counts are recorded per division in the registry, with a stated basis.** The basis is:
  - a standard edition's count where that is well established
  - otherwise, the source's own consecutive numbering, cross-checked against the commentary page
- **Śaṅkara's bhāṣyas** are fetched from their own Wikisource pages (`उपनिषद्भाष्यम्` and its links). They are split per mantra by each page's own markers.
  - No school notes for the Śvetāśvatara: its bhāṣya page doesn't exist, and Śaṅkara's authorship of it is disputed.
  - No school notes for the Kauṣītaki or Maitrī: Śaṅkara did not comment on them.
- **Vedic signs in IAST:** ꣳ (the Vedic 'gum' nasal) → `ṁ`; spacing candrabindu → `m̐`. Accent marks are dropped from IAST; the Devanagari keeps them.
- **The Gita annotations glob is now `ch[0-9][0-9].json`.** Plain `ch*.json` would also match `chandogya_upanishad.json`.
- **Verses that could read as being about suicide** (e.g. Isha 3, "those who slay the self"):
  - No situations, so they are never offered as advice.
  - They are flagged `death` and `distress_gentle`, even when the commentary reads them figuratively.
- **Ritual and invocation passages** (peace chants, colophons) are skipped by the parsers, as the Gita's chapter colophons are. Only numbered units become records.

## 2026-10-02: Answer engine, safety and privacy

- **Safety rule revised (by the user):**
  - Never recommend renunciation, abandoning responsibilities, or fatalism to someone in distress or under 18.
  - Always support leaving abuse or danger, and point to help.
  - Help numbers live in `data/help_resources.json`, each with its source and check date (2026-10-02):
    - India: 112, Tele-MANAS 14416, Childline 1098, women's helpline 181
    - US: 988
    - UK: Samaritans 116 123
- **Privacy check of the AI provider (Anthropic API terms, checked 2026-10-02):**
  - API inputs and outputs are not used for training by default.
  - They *are* stored for up to 30 days, and for up to 2 years if trust-and-safety systems flag them.
  - Zero-data-retention agreements exist, subject to Anthropic's approval, but flagged content and legal holds are still exempt.
  - Sources: privacy.claude.com, "How long do you store my organization's data?" (updated 2026-07-01), and docs.anthropic.com, "API and data retention".
  - **Result:** the default API terms do NOT meet the promise that session context is never saved.
- **Decision on the engine:**
  - The answer engine runs locally, with deterministic retrieval and templates, and sends nothing to any provider.
  - An optional LLM step (Claude) exists in the code but is off by default, and is blocked unless the setting `VIVEKA_PROVIDER_ZDR=1` declares that a zero-data-retention agreement is in place.
  - Even then, the privacy notice must disclose the flagged-content exception.
  - Signing a ZDR agreement is a contract and cost decision for the user.
- **Drafts stay internal.**
  - The engine has two modes: `public` (only records with `review_status` reviewed or approved) and `internal` (drafts allowed, every answer marked DRAFT).
  - No record is reviewed yet, so tests run in internal mode. Public mode currently returns an honest "no reviewed material yet" answer.
- **Answer engine design (2026-10-02):**
  - Problem types ("frames", in `engine/frames.py`) pick candidate principles, then situation tags and word overlap rank them.
  - Danger restricts the candidates to safety-and-agency principles.
  - Self-harm (the person's own or someone else's) skips philosophy entirely, giving care and help lines only.
  - Distress adds a support note and gentle principles.
  - Restricted principles and held verses are never shown to anyone not known to be an adult, or to anyone in distress.
  - A clarifying question is asked only if re-running the engine as if for an adult would change the top recommendation, or when the person gave too little to go on.
- **Always-on safety footer (2026-10-02):** every answer ends with help lines. Detection missed crisis language in held-out tests, so a safety net that does not depend on detection is required.
- **Test integrity:**
  - The engine's author also wrote the tests, so every held-out set is scored once before any changes, and that first score is the one reported.
  - Sets used for tuning are marked as spent in `tests/README.md`.
- **Nitishataka (2026-10-02):**
  - Source: the Wikisource page `नीतिशतकम्`.
  - The two unnumbered opening verses (an invocation, and the famous 'yāṃ cintayāmi') are not units, following the rule that only numbered units become records. The source has no verse 7; `missing_units` in the registry records this, and the validator accepts it.
  - **New flag `fatalism`:** marks verses saying outcomes are fixed by fate or past deeds. The engine never shows them to anyone in distress or not known to be an adult.
  - **Verses that demean women or rank people by birth** (e.g. 'boldness with women', royal policy likened to a courtesan) are flagged `caste_gender`. Their English stays faithful.
  - Verses calling the unlearned 'beasts' are flagged `distress_gentle`.
  - **Transliteration:** nukta letters such as ड़ are now handled (needed for Hindi and Awadhi texts too).
